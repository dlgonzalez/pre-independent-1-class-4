Vocabulary: The Anagram Challenge

An anagram is a word made by rearranging the letters of another word. To solve these, you need to read the definition, figure out the first word, and then mix the letters to solve the second!

How it works

  • Read Clue A and type the word.
  • Read Clue B. It uses the exact same letters as Clue A!
  • Click "Check" to see if you solved the riddle. Solve all 20!

Example:

Clue A: The hair that covers a man's face. ➡️ B E A R D

Clue B: Food that is baked from dough. ➡️ B R E A D

Expressing Ability

In the audio, we heard phrases like "he *could* play" and "he *managed to* learn". Why didn't they just use "can" for everything? Let's decode the grammar of abilities.

The Timeline of Ability ⏳

🟢

General Present

Things you know how to do right now.

can + base verb
"I can speak English."
am/is/are able to + base verb
"She is able to solve the puzzle."
⏮️

General Past

Things you knew how to do in the past.

could + base verb
"Mozart could play the violin."
was/were able to + base verb
"They were able to swim well."
🔥
🏆

Difficult Achievement

Doing something specific that was hard to achieve. (Do NOT use 'could' for a single successful past event!).

manage to + base verb
"I managed to finish the marathon."
succeed in + verb-ing
"He succeeded in learning Icelandic."

Everyday "Extreme" Survival Podcast

We usually think of survival as life-or-death, but real life is full of mundane disasters! Let's use our new grammar to talk about surviving the absolute worst daily situations.

Step-by-Step

  1. Pick a nightmare: Choose one of the 6 Everyday Disasters below.
  2. Prepare your drama: Exaggerate the story! Plan how you survived using manage to, succeed in, and was able to.
  3. Record the Interview: Partner A is the Podcast Host. Partner B is the Survivor. Play your roles with lots of emotion! Then switch.

👶 The Crying Baby

You are on a 12-hour flight seated next to a baby who will NOT stop screaming.

🪑 The IKEA Monster

You are assembling a massive piece of IKEA furniture, but you lost the manual.

🦃 The Broken Oven

You are cooking a huge Thanksgiving dinner for 15 people and the oven breaks.

☕ The Coffee Spill

You spilled coffee all over your only white shirt 5 minutes before a huge job interview.

🚪 The Locked Door

You took the trash out in your pajamas and the apartment door locked behind you.

🚗 The Family Roadtrip

A 5-hour family car trip with a broken AC and a phone that just died (no GPS).

Host Questions 🎙️

1. The Setup

"So, tell our listeners... what exactly was the nightmare situation you found yourself in?"

2. The Breaking Point

"It sounds horrible. Were you able to stay calm, or were you losing your mind?"

3. The Breakthrough

"How on earth did you manage to fix the problem?"

4. The End

"In the end, did you succeed in finishing what you started?"

🎭 Sound Natural!

Use these filler phrases to make your story sound dramatic and real:

  • "To be honest, it was a total nightmare..."
  • "You won't believe it, but..."
  • "I was completely losing my mind!"
  • "By some miracle, I..."
View Sample Transcript

Host: So, how did you manage to survive the IKEA bed assembly without the manual?

Survivor: To be honest, it was a total nightmare. I couldn't understand any of the pieces. But eventually, after crying for ten minutes, I managed to find a tutorial on YouTube.

Host: Incredible! Did you succeed in putting it together?

Survivor: You won't believe it, but yes! I succeeded in building the frame. Because of that video, I was able to sleep on a real bed that night!
